Clients — two directions

Client list, client view and edit client, shown on iPhone in light appearance with fictional data. Both stay inside the shipped Daybook system: SF Pro, forest on ivory, ruled panels, capsule glass actions. They differ in what the client screens are for.

A · Account ledger The client as a running account: who owes what, and what's next.

Every list row answers "do they owe me, and when do I work for them next?". The client view leads with a currency-split standing panel, then one combined history where each event carries its money state, so invoices no longer sit in a separate disconnected list. The editor is grouped by purpose, with presets for terms and currency and a live preview of the bill-to block that will appear on invoices.

B · Daybook roster The client as a working relationship, read through the calendar.

The list borrows the agenda's date rail so clients sort by when you'll see them next, with an A–Z index for lookup. The client view mirrors the event page: a compact header with fact chips, then Events / Money / Details tabs, so long histories and invoice lists stop competing on one scroll. The editor keeps native label-and-value rows (the same pattern as the invoicing sheet) and shows invoicing readiness as a small meter.
